01 · What it isL’Après is the after-party of the market.

Nights of Wonder is RASA’s family holiday market under the Transamerica Pyramid, open 6 to 10 PM. On Fridays and Saturdays, once the market closes at 10, the AMA space turns into L’Après: an adults-only, alpine-styled Hi-Fi Listening Lodge until 2 AM. “Après” as in après-ski, the drinks after the slopes. Here it’s the night after the market.

Each night is an L’Après Session: a guest artist or selector playing records, a local mixologist with a signature cocktail matched to the sound, and stories behind the music. Entry is by RSVP with a cash bar, for about 200 people through the night. In RASA’s deck the room was sketched as “Château Après” with the same lodge line on a backlit sign over the door, which is why the logo has to work in neon or backlit.

03 · The new referencesWhat fits L’Après, and what doesn’t

L’Après is an alpine listening lodge: records, cocktails, a door sign that lights up. A reference fits if it helps the logo say sound, mountain or Parisian night in type that can be cut into a sign. The board is great for flyers too; that plan is further down.

Works, and we used it

  • PulseClub: a small sound symbol sitting on a serif name. It became the EQ bars drawn as two Alpine peaks (P1, P3) and the waveform that turns into a mountain (P2).
  • Camel and the Jimmy J monogram: meaning cut into the letters. It became the snowline through the caps (N1), the A as a summit (N2) and the spark filling the L’s corner (N3).
  • A Main Levée and the After Hours poster: tall condensed couture serifs. It became the condensed family K1 to K3.
  • The logo-concept sheet (Aura, Elevate, Lucid): one quiet symbol over spaced capitals, and the arch. It became the arch door (K4) and the symbol-over-wordmark layout of P1 and P4.
  • Jimmy J’s stationery and the Lucky for Season box: a logo living on objects. It became the room key fob (N4) and points to the mockups: key tags, coasters, matchbooks, coat-check tickets.
  • DJ Santa “Happy New Year”, the Santa flash portraits, Santa on the decks: these belong on the Sessions flyers (see Next).

Doesn’t fit the logo

  • Static and Street Club: brush scripts. Streetwear energy, and you asked for no cursive.
  • Clubhouse blob figures and Tokyo 3D type: playful and cartoonish, too young for a lodge.
  • Clip-art logo grids (Right Work, GD House, AgriFlix) and Event Experts: generic gradients and icons. They would cheapen it.
  • Sprite and the burger ad: fast-food retail, wrong world.
  • Streetwear emblem sheet: the condensed type is useful, but the stamp-emblem look reads as merch, not a night out.
  • Muhwano, SK Studio, the Strategy portrait and the app icons: brand-system examples rather than a look for L’Après.

Round 2 · Your notesWhat changed from Round 1

These were the notes on Round 1, which is kept at the bottom of the page.

My favourite is the Spark accent.

The spark over the E is now the core of every logo, from the wordmark to the flags, seals and icon.

Remove the cursive ones.

The scripts are gone. Everything is set in capitals or a serif lowercase.

Don’t do the tonearm apostrophe.

Removed. Every mark uses the font’s own apostrophe: L’APRÈS, never LAPRÈS.

I liked “Hi-Fi Listening Lodge”.

It’s now the standing line, set between hairlines like the door sign in RASA’s deck.

I like the flags.

Four flags: a felt pennant, a pennant with the lodge line, a swallowtail burgee and a single-line pennant for neon.

The circles need more separation.

Wider gaps between rings, arc text and the centre, and fewer elements in each seal.
